## Why This Case Matters

Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo sits near the center of OLI's mission.

The formal issue was whether Chevron deference should be overruled or clarified. The deeper issue was whether courts must exercise independent judgment about the meaning of law or defer to agencies whenever a statute is ambiguous.

For OLI, this is a core objective-law problem. If legal obligations shift with agency policy, then citizens do not live under knowable law. They live under mutable administrative command.

## The Legal Question

Chevron required courts, in many circumstances, to defer to an agency's reasonable interpretation of an ambiguous statute.

The problem is that ambiguity is often where legal judgment matters most. If the agency that enforces a statute also receives deference in defining its own power, then the judicial function is partly transferred to the executive branch.

## The OLI Angle

An OLI analysis would strongly support judicial independence in statutory interpretation.

Courts must identify the objective meaning of law in context. They may consider agency expertise where relevant to facts, technical background, or implementation. But the legal meaning of a statute is not whatever the enforcing agency reasonably prefers.

Individuals need law that can be read, argued, judged, and applied by independent courts. Without that, planning becomes conditional on shifting bureaucratic interpretation.

## What OLI Could Have Contributed

OLI could have contributed a philosophical and jurisprudential account of why deference doctrines are not merely institutional conveniences.

Deference can become a mechanism by which legal meaning is detached from enacted law and attached to administrative will. The rule of law requires that courts say what the law is because citizens need objective legal standards to govern their actions.

## Why Timely Support Matters

Loper Bright is the kind of case OLI should be prepared for. It implicates administrative power, separation of powers, judicial review, legal objectivity, and individual planning all at once.

With support, OLI can develop standing frameworks for post-Chevron litigation and help shape the next generation of administrative-law doctrine.

## Clarification

OLI's concern is not that administrative agencies have no legitimate role. The principle is that legal meaning belongs to law, not to agency preference, and courts must exercise independent judgment when interpreting statutes.
